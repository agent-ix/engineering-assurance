# Structural coverage

Structural coverage asks whether every item in a declared project-owned set has
an explicit outcome. It is an accounting discipline, not a quality score.

A coverage scope must identify:

- the owned set and the revision that defines it;
- the event or decision that makes an item applicable;
- the evidence that can discharge it;
- permitted disposition states and their accountable owners; and
- the rule proving that every item appears exactly once.

Unknown, unreadable, excluded, and intentionally deferred items remain visible
as separate outcomes. They must not be silently merged into success or removed
from the denominator.

Projects own their disposition vocabulary. A consumer may relate its local
states to this pattern, but it must not claim the vocabularies are identical
without a separately reviewed compatibility change.
